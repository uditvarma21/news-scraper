Role of Civil Services in a Democracy

Special Executive Magistrates

29-10-2026

Source: TH

Why in News?  

A Special Executive Magistrate (SEM) court in Delhi granted bail to two activists, rejecting the police’s request for extended custody over an alleged breach of peace bonds. The case highlights the preventive powers, jurisdiction and role of SEMs compared with Judicial Magistrates.

Who are Special Executive Magistrates (SEMs)? 

  • Appointment: Under Section 15 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS), 2023, State Governments may appoint Executive Magistrates or police officers not below the rank of Superintendent of Police (SP) or equivalent as SEMs for specified areas, periods or functions. 
  • Police as Magistrates: In commissionerate systems like Delhi, the Delhi Police Act, 1978 allows the Centre to authorise police officers of the rank of Assistant Commissioner of Police (ACP) and above to exercise the powers of an executive magistrate. 
  • Nature of Role: SEMs belong to the Executive branch. Their primary function is strictly preventive and administrative to intervene before a disturbance happens, rather than to punish an individual for an offence that has already been committed. 
  • Key Powers of SEMs The powers of SEMs are largely preventive and are codified under the BNSS, 2023: 
    • Issuing "Peace Bonds" (Section 126, BNSS): If an SEM suspects a person is likely to breach public peace, they can ask the individual to show cause as to why they should not execute a formal bond promising good behaviour for a period of up to one year. 
    • Preventive Inquiry (Section 135, BNSS): The magistrate can conduct an inquiry and order security during the process, provided they record their reasons in writing. 
    • Detention for Breach (Section 141(1)(b), BNSS): If an individual breaks the conditions of their peace bond, the SEM can bypass a normal trial and order their immediate arrest and detention for the remaining period of the bond. 
    • Urgent Prohibitory Orders (Section 163, BNSS): SEMs can issue urgent preventive orders against nuisance or apprehended danger to human life, health, safety, or public tranquillity (e.g., preventing riots or obstruction). 
    • Bail in Preventive Crackdowns: While they do not handle ordinary criminal bail, SEMs have the authority to accept sureties and grant bail or order temporary police custody during preventive proceedings. 
  • Judicial Review: The actions and orders of an SEM are entirely subject to judicial review and administrative oversight to ensure they are legal and that citizens' rights are not arbitrarily violated. 
  • Right to Challenge: Depending on the nature of the specific order and the legal remedies available, a citizen can challenge an SEM's decision before an appropriate higher court or authority. 

What are the Concerns Regarding the Powers of SEMs? 

  • Threat to Personal Liberty: Preventive arrests without a formal trial raise severe concerns regarding Article 21 (Right to Life and Personal Liberty) and Article 22 (Protection against arbitrary arrest and detention) of the Constitution. 
  • Mechanical Orders and "Cyclostyled" Proformas: Courts have repeatedly flagged the indiscriminate use of SEM powers.  
  • Procedural Safeguards and Accountability: In Aldanish Rein v. State of NCT of Delhi (2018), the Delhi High Court emphasised reasoned orders, periodic detention reviews, affordable sureties, access to legal aid and independent oversight to prevent arbitrary exercise of SEM powers. 
  • Colonial Legacy: The preventive powers of SEMs trace their origins back to England's Justices of the Peace Act, 1361, which entered Indian law in 1861 to suppress dissent, retaining a colonial overtone. 
  • Conflict of Interest and Natural Justice: When police officers act as SEMs, the same executive machinery may initiate preventive proceedings and adjudicate them, raising concerns under the principle of Nemo Judex in Causa Sua (no one should be a judge in their own cause) and Article 50 (separation of judiciary from executive).

SEMs vs. Judicial Magistrates

Feature 

Special Executive Magistrates (SEMs) 

Judicial Magistrates 

Appointment 

Appointed by the State Government . 

Courts of Judicial Magistrates (first and second class) are established under Section 9 of the BNSS. While the State determines their number and location, the presiding officers are appointed by the High Court, not the State government. 

Primary Role 

Administrative and preventive (maintaining law and order). 

Judicial Magistrates are an independent part of the judicial system tasked with dealing with formal criminal cases, hearing regular bail applications, conducting trials, and imposing legal sentences. 

Judicial Powers 

Cannot conduct formal criminal trials, convict, or impose sentences. 

Presides over criminal courts, handles trials, and sentences offenders. 

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs) 

1. What is a Special Executive Magistrate (SEM)?
A SEM is an executive authority appointed by the government under Section 15 of the BNSS, 2023, to exercise specified magisterial powers for maintaining public order and preventing breaches of peace. 

2. What are the key preventive powers of SEMs under the BNSS, 2023?
Their powers include requiring security for keeping the peace under Section 126, conducting inquiries under Section 135 and issuing urgent orders under Section 163, subject to applicable legal conditions. 

3. How do Special Executive Magistrates differ from Judicial Magistrates?
SEMs primarily exercise executive and preventive powers, while Judicial Magistrates handle criminal proceedings, conduct trials within their jurisdiction and exercise judicial powers independently. 

4. How do SEM powers raise concerns regarding fundamental rights?
Preventive proceedings and detention may affect Article 21 (right to life and personal liberty) and Article 22 (safeguards against arrest and detention), making procedural safeguards and judicial review essential.

5. Why is the separation of powers relevant to SEMs?
When police officers exercise magisterial powers, concerns may arise over impartiality and accountability. Article 50 directs the State to separate the judiciary from the executive in public services.

Cabinet Secretary of India

27-10-2026

Source: IE 

Why in News?  

The Cabinet Secretariat declined to initiate disciplinary action against a senior IAS officer serving in the Election Commission of India (ECI), following separate complaints by two Election Commissioners. 

  • The Secretariat cited the ECI’s constitutional autonomy, noting that the complaints were made in the commissioners’ individual capacities rather than through a formal ECI decision, bringing the powers and jurisdictional limits of the Cabinet Secretary into focus. 

What is the Cabinet Secretariat? 

  • About: The Cabinet Secretariat is an apex administrative office that operates directly under the Prime Minister of India.  
    • It is responsible for handling paperwork, setting the agenda, recording decisions, and ensuring follow-up for the Union Cabinet. Structurally, it consists of three wings: 
      • Civil Wing: The primary wing that assists the Cabinet and manages business rules. 
      • Military Wing: Coordinates defence-related matters. 
      • Intelligence Wing: Oversees bodies like the Research and Analysis Wing (R&AW), which was set up in 1968 as a wing of the Cabinet Secretariat. 
  • Origin: The Cabinet Secretariat evolved from the portfolio system established under the Indian Councils Act, 1861 (enacted under Lord Canning).  
    • Initially, the Viceroy’s Private Secretary headed the Executive Council Secretariat. Lord Willingdon introduced the practice of having the Private Secretary attend meetings. 
    • By 1935, the Private Secretary was also designated Secretary to the Executive Council, though the roles were later separated.  
    • In September 1946, it was renamed the Cabinet Secretariat, and its head became the Cabinet Secretary.  
      • Gopalaswami Ayyangar Report (1949), which emphasised the need for a highly senior, impartial Cabinet Secretary capable of ensuring effective coordination among government departments. 
    • The office was formally established in independent India in 1950, with N.R. Pillai was appointed as the country's first Cabinet Secretary. 
  • Constitutional and Legal Basis: While the post of Cabinet Secretary is not explicitly mentioned in the Constitution of India, the Secretariat's functions are primarily governed by the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961, and the Transaction of Business Rules, 1961.  
    • These rules are framed by the President under Article 77(3) of the Constitution. 
  • Attached Offices and Organisations: The Cabinet Secretariat is associated with institutions such as the Directorate of Public Grievances (DPG), National Authority for the Chemical Weapons Convention, Office of the Principal Scientific Adviser (PSA) and Direct Benefit Transfer (DBT) Mission, supporting public grievance redressal, international treaty obligations, scientific advice and welfare delivery.

Who is the Cabinet Secretary?

  • About: The Cabinet Secretary is the administrative head of the Cabinet Secretariat and the senior-most civil servant in the Government of India. It is the highest position an Indian Administrative Service (IAS) officer can hold while in active service. 
  • Current Incumbent: T. V. Somanathan, a 1987-batch IAS officer of the Tamil Nadu cadre, has served as India's 32nd Cabinet Secretary since 30th August 2024. His tenure was extended in August 2026 until August 2027. 
  • Order of Precedence: The official ranks 11th on the Indian Order of Precedence. 
  • Tenure:  The tenure of the Cabinet Secretary is typically fixed at two years. 
    • Under the All India Services (Death-cum-Retirement Benefits) Rules, 1958 (Rule 56(d)), the central government can grant extensions.  
    • In 2010, the maximum limit was extended to four years, and a 2019 amendment allowed an additional extension of up to one year in the "public interest". 
      • Currently, a Cabinet Secretary can serve a maximum total tenure of five years. 
    • The Cabinet Secretary is exempt from standard superannuation rules, allowing them to serve beyond the retirement age of 60 until their prescribed tenure expires.  
  • Ex-Officio Roles: The Cabinet Secretary serves as the ex-officio Chairman of the Civil Services Board and the National Crisis Management Committee (NCMC). 
  • Key Functions and Powers: 
    • Secretarial Assistance to the Cabinet: The core function involves preparing the agenda for Cabinet meetings, circulating relevant papers, recording discussions, and tracking the implementation of Cabinet decisions. 
    • Monitoring and Administrative Oversight: The Cabinet Secretariat monitors the implementation of Cabinet decisions and receives monthly reports from Ministries/Departments on major policy developments, implementation issues, inter-ministerial coordination and compliance with government procedures. 
    • Crisis Management: During national emergencies or major crises, the Cabinet Secretary coordinates the activities of various ministries to ensure administrative stability and continuity. 
    • Cadre Management and Appointments: As the head of the civil services, the official handles all senior administrative appointments in the central government and serves as a "conscience keeper" to protect civil servants from political victimization. 
    • Union-State Liaison: The Cabinet Secretary convenes the Annual Conference of Chief Secretaries to harmonize the execution of policies and government schemes between the Centre and the States. 
  • Limits of the Office’s Powers: 
    • No Jurisdiction Over Constitutional Bodies: The Cabinet Secretariat cannot intervene in the internal administrative or disciplinary matters of independent bodies like the ECI. Bypassing these bodies' internal mechanisms is outside its legal purview. 
    • Administrative, Not Political: The office holds no independent political portfolio. It acts purely as a neutral, executive facilitator operating under the Prime Minister’s direction. 
    • Bound by Business Rules: The office operates strictly within the Government of India (Allocation and Transaction of Business) Rules, 1961. It coordinates government functions but cannot override the statutory authority of line ministries. 
    • Risk of Overlap: Its coordination functions often overlap with the Prime Minister's Office (PMO) and the National Security Council Secretariat (NSCS), which can occasionally blur lines of responsibility. 

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs) 

1. What is the Cabinet Secretariat and under whose authority does it function? 
The Cabinet Secretariat functions directly under the Prime Minister of India and facilitates Cabinet decision-making, inter-ministerial coordination and implementation of government decisions. 

2. What is the constitutional basis of the Cabinet Secretariat?
The Cabinet Secretary’s post is not explicitly mentioned in the Constitution. The Secretariat’s functions are governed primarily by the Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961, and the Transaction of Business Rules, 1961, framed under Article 77(3).

3. What is the tenure of the Cabinet Secretary?
The normal tenure is two years, with extensions permitted under applicable service rules. The maximum tenure can extend to five years, subject to the prescribed rules.

4. What are the major functions of the Cabinet Secretary?
The Cabinet Secretary coordinates Cabinet meetings, resolves inter-ministerial differences, supports crisis management and serves as the ex-officio Chairman of the Civil Services Board and National Crisis Management Committee.

Special Campaign 5.0-Towards Efficient Governance in India

23-10-2025

Source: PIB 

Special Campaign 5.0 is making headlines as its implementation phase(October 2–31, 2025) is underway across Indian government offices. Ministries have reported significant progress in clearing public grievances. 

Special Campaign 5.0

  • The Special Campaign 5.0 (officially, the Special Campaign for Disposal of Pending Matters 5.0 or SCDPM 5.0) is an initiative of the Government of India led by the Department of Administrative Reforms and Public Grievances (DARPG). 
    • Building on the success of earlier editions, Campaign 5.0 focuses on “Minimizing Pendency and Institutionalizing Swachhata.” 
  • Primary Objectives: 
    • Reduce pendency by expediting disposal of public grievances, appeals, and inter-ministerial references. 
    • Review and close old physical and e-files to promote digital efficiency. 
    • Conduct cleanliness drives in workplaces and public-facing offices. 
    • Ensure scientific e-waste and scrap disposal under the E-Waste Management Rules, 2022.

18th National Civil Services Day

21-04-2026

Source: TH

On the occasion of 18th National Civil Services Day, the Prime Minister extended greetings to civil servants and emphasized the need to strengthen their commitment to good governance and nation-building, highlighting their vital role from grassroots implementation to policymaking in driving India’s progress with excellence, compassion, and innovation. 

  • Every year on this day, the Prime Minister confers the Prime Minister’s Awards for Excellence in Public Administration (PMAEPA) on districts and implementing units for achievements in priority programme implementation and innovation.  
  • National Civil Services Day: It is observed on 21st April each year to honor the dedication of civil servants. First celebrated in 2006, it commemorates Sardar Vallabhbhai Patel’s address to the probationers of Administrative Services Officers at Metcalf House in Delhi, where he referred to the civil servants as the “steel frame of India” on 21st April 1947. 
  • PMAEPA: To acknowledge, recognize, and reward the extraordinary and innovative work done by districts and organizations of the Central and State Governments, the Prime Minister’s Awards for Excellence in Public Administration(PMAEPA) are conferred. 
    • The selection process includes evaluation by a Screening Committee, Expert Committee, and final approval by the Cabinet Secretary and Prime Minister. 
    • The award includes a trophy, a scroll, and Rs 20 lakh to support public welfare initiatives. 
  • Initiatives Related to Civil Services: Mission Karmayogi, Lateral Entry Scheme (LES), e-Samiksha, and Centralized Public Grievance Redress and Monitoring System (CPGRAMS).
